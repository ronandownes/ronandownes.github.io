The model

From individual behaviour to population outcomes

Rather than assuming everyone behaves alike, an agent-based model represents individuals, their ages, relationships, infection states and vaccination histories. Population patterns emerge from many individual interactions.

1. Individuals & networks

Agents form and end relationships; contacts create opportunities for HPV transmission.

2. Vaccination policies

Compare routine vaccination, optional catch-up campaigns and interruptions to programmes.

3. Long-term scenarios

Simulate up to 100 years and compare trajectories over repeated stochastic runs.

Experimental design

What difference do interventions make?

The model is designed to compare policies under consistent assumptions. Outcomes are assessed across repeated simulations, rather than presenting a single run as a prediction.

100-year experimental horizon

1,200 monthly model steps. The longer horizon allows investigation of delayed effects, population turnover and potential re-emergence of infection.

Important: a scenario is not a validated forecast. Future results will include assumptions, uncertainty and independent checks.

Scope and limitations

The present inherited model focuses on HPV types 16 and 18 and includes assumptions from historical literature. Irish vaccination rates and the full spectrum of cervical cancer outcomes cannot be inferred directly from uncalibrated simulation runs. The project separates observed evidence, modelling assumptions and exploratory projections.
